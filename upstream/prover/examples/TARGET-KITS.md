# The example configurations (prover/examples/target_*)

Six configurations for cp_commit, cp_prove and cp_verify (the example script's messages call such a folder a kit). A configuration holds settings and no weights: `run_target_example.sh` runs the programs on [synthetic weights](../../docs/glossary.md#synthetic-weights) (`made-up` in options and output). The programs know one field, p = 2^64 - 2^32 + 1, with plain Reed-Solomon codes; every parameter file here begins with the line `version H821`.

Masking is built in and on by default. (Weight hiding: [guide/weight-hiding.md](../../guide/weight-hiding.md).)

| folder | what it is |
|---|---|
| target_small_f2 | a small model: 64 tokens, 8 layers. The default `cp_commit` makes no commitment at it: its worst-case check ends with status 7 (the end of this page); to run the programs take target_small_f2_roomy |
| target_small_f2_roomy | target_small_f2 with roomier scales (a KL.bin made in one round by a script that is not part of this repository; policy.txt's line MF 43 for 44): the worst-case check passes on it. Its [README.md](target_small_f2_roomy/README.md) says in plain words what a YES rests on |
| target_small_f2_gap | One of the two configurations with an acceptance policy other than the [exact match](../../docs/glossary.md#exact-match) (the other: target_full_f2_gap): target_small_f2_roomy's policy.txt with three other lines (`C`, `theta`, `cap`), which set a [clipped gap](../../docs/glossary.md#clipped-gap); its layout.txt; a KL.bin of its own. The limits were fitted on replies of a small floating-point model exported from made-up weights: they fit this example only and say nothing about a real model's thresholds. With one honest reply and one control that misses the limits ([target_small_f2_gap/README.md](target_small_f2_gap/README.md)) |
| target_full_f2 | the full-size model: 1,024 tokens, 48 layers |
| target_full_f2_gap | target_full_f2 with a clipped gap as its acceptance policy: target_full_f2's policy.txt with three other lines (`C`, `theta`, `cap`); its layout.txt and KL.bin are copies of target_full_f2's. `C` and `theta` are round multiples of 2^sh = 256, the constant that the programs add to every logit gap (`C` 25856 = 101 x 256, `theta` 512 = 2 x 256), and `cap` is 1: they were fitted on nothing and say nothing about a real model's thresholds. It has no request file |
| target_small_file_f2 | For tests on a model's files alone: a small model of its own (256 tokens, 4 layers). It has no layout.txt: a test makes one. Its parameter file was written without a tool (the whole account stands below the table). Of the tests of hiding the first alone has run at it, once on the released programs (docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row LIM-63.2, line 11). |

<a id="weight-hiding"></a>**Weight hiding: read this before you run a configuration on weights that matter.**

Every stored table has OOD answers (the line `record 1`: "The table's OOD answers", below).

- Which settings, files and weights the tests of hiding covered, and which they did not: [guide/weight-hiding.md](../../guide/weight-hiding.md) and [docs/limits.md](../../docs/limits.md#not-covered) ("Not covered").
- The programs take 8- or 16-bit files without a warning ([why](../../docs/limits.md#files-of-8-or-16-bits)), and `-public-weights` hides nothing. A provider that serves for long enough, or on a second GPU, needs a further commitment to the same weights ([how](../../docs/operators-duties.md#a-new-commitment)). Above all, each commitment must sample its own secret seed, on a machine that was not cloned or restored. Each must also take nothing from another commitment and keep the same configuration files ([all the conditions](../../docs/limits.md#a-further-commitment)). Two commitments to the same weights under different acceptance policies have different configuration files, so together they do not meet this condition. No test has searched two commitments together for leaks.
- What is not hidden by design: the [declared list](../../docs/what-is-not-hidden.md).
- `cp_prove` runs no verifier on the proofs that it writes: let no response leave you before `cp_verify` has accepted every proof of its batch ([duty 6](../../docs/limits.md#the-conditions)).
- The five tests of hiding ran on synthetic weights but for four runs of the first, one of them on the released programs, nearly all at small size; two of the five ran on the released programs, the other three on [other programs](../../docs/glossary.md#other-programs), two of these on test builds of other sources ([which](../../docs/how-hiding-is-checked.md)). Audits apart, no test of hiding ran under a clipped-gap policy. At target_small_f2 the tests of hiding ran on test builds under a test seed, which leaves cp_commit's worst-case check out; on the released programs the first and the fifth ran at target_small_f2_roomy, under seeds that cp_commit drew, the first also at target_small_file_f2 and at full size, at target_full_f2 and in files fitted to a real model (docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row HW-747), and both audits once more at target_small_f2_gap (docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row H13.8). On [test builds](../../docs/glossary.md#test-build) of other sources, at the [default setting](../../docs/glossary.md#default-setting)'s small form, 30 of 165 picked losses in CPU memory went unnoticed (12 of the 165 changed no byte of any file, so 30 of the 153 that could show anything), and none of 130 in GPU memory; these are picked cases, so no rate follows (docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row LIM-63.2).
- What the OOD answers ask of hiding. The answers are linear forms of the weights, published for good; each is masked by a pad that the row holds (docs/format/COIN-KEYS.md, "Below a table's commit-run key"; prover/hash_commitment/hf_pads.cuh). One known limit: if a commit-run key comes twice over different weights, the two commitments' public files alone give, for every row of each table, one exact linear equation in the two sets of weights, for good (COIN-KEYS.md, section 3; docs/operators-duties.md).

  Two stored tables of one commitment are proved equal in every entry but their pads and random entries, which each table draws for itself and which [the link](../../docs/glossary.md#link) does not compare. Every proof that reads a table holds them to that table's own root and published answers, and to nothing of another table. No verifier can check that they are random. By a reading of the code, one [self-check](../../docs/glossary.md#self-check) in cp_commit is all that stands between a fault that loses every pad and a public file without pads. In a trial on a test build (without proof of work, which the default setting counts), with that check switched off, a commitment without pads passed everything else that cp_commit does; that trial ran once.

  Whoever gets the [seed](../../docs/glossary.md#seed) (for a [leaf table](../../docs/glossary.md#leaf-table): with its kept bytes) can open every published answer, at any time, with no proof served. The danger ends only when the seed and the leaf tables' secrets are gone, backups too, and this repository has no tool that destroys them. (In the secret key directory the seed is `seed.bin`, and a leaf table's kept bytes are `leaf/<k>/fresh.bin`.)

*target_small_file_f2: what it is.* For tests on a model's files alone: a small model of its own (256 tokens, 4 layers; model.txt holds the numbers from which make_model writes two GGUF parts of 530 MB in all). No layout.txt: a test makes it as below, with the numbers 17 and 4 (17: log2 of the link group's message length; 4, by two readings of these sources: the entries left empty at the end of each row, for the pad).

Its policy.txt is in the standard parameter form, because the programs take no option for tests on a model's files. It was written without a tool. cp_commit's worst-case check passes on make_model's files under its scales as they are (seen once). Its lines `record`, `work positions`, `group` and `stages` are the default's, as at the other small configurations, made for another small model's arrays; the line `table 8 26 7864320 524287 0 14` is its own and makes room for the pads: the [planning call](../../docs/glossary.md#planning-call) takes the file, but these groups have not been chosen for this table.

The public test `gpu.record_on_public_weights` (SUITE.md) commits, proves one request and verifies under it, on make_model's files, once under a seed that cp_commit draws and once with `-public-weights`, under which nothing is hidden; on the released programs it has run once and passed (reported). A pass says nothing about what the OOD answers hide: one leaf table, one request, small size.

## The setting

**The default** is policy.txt in target_full_f2, target_full_f2_gap, target_small_f2, target_small_f2_roomy, target_small_f2_gap and target_small_file_f2. Every test of closeness to a code goes up to the [Johnson radius](../../docs/glossary.md#johnson-radius) of that code (rule 1 in the lines `group`). That holds for what a request and a batch send, and for what the committer sends once, in key and entry.

[Proof of work](../../docs/glossary.md#proof-of-work) is counted. The line `work positions 20 28 32` asks for a nonce search before positions are drawn, and nowhere else. The search is of 20 bits in the chains that belong to one request, of 28 bits in the chain that all requests of a batch have in common, and of 32 bits in key and entry.

An honest call ends YES (status 0; a part-call PART PASSED, 3; seen at target_small_f2_roomy's and target_full_f2's files: "What has run under it", below), and a line before the last says that work was counted: `proof of work: up to 32 bits counted in <m> rounds; with it the least round has <a> bits, without it <b> bits` (each a single round's figure, neither a security level).

At target_small_f2_gap and target_full_f2_gap a call that names the configuration's clipped-gap policy and covers a token of the request ends `WITHIN LIMITS: clipped-gap ..`, status 0 too, which is no YES.

**What a YES does not say.** A YES from `cp_verify` does not say that the weights were hidden.

**Another parameter file.** The programs read the file named policy.txt in the folder that they are given, and no other. The example script takes only a folder whose name begins with target_ and that lies beside it in prover/examples/: so whoever wants to try a file of their own lays a copy of a folder there under such a name, with that file as its policy.txt, or calls cp_commit, cp_prove and cp_verify on a folder of their own.

Build first, or lay the folder in a second copy of the repository. The reason: every file below prover/, pages and tests apart, goes into the digest of the sources that prover/build.sh writes into a program's build record (`<program>.build`), which an operator compares. So a build that is made while your folder lies there records another digest than a build from a clean checkout.

Before it creates anything, the script looks at the available host memory wherever the configuration is larger than the small examples: it goes by the configuration's size, which it reads from policy.txt (the second and third words of the line `table`). A commitment is bound to its parameter file: another file gives another commitment, with an ID of its own.

**What it costs, by the planning call** (`cp_verify -plan accounting <folder> 128`; averages over the drawn positions). At full size a proof has 18.78 MB (at most 19,254,524 bytes), a key 3.29 MB (at most 3,325,152) and an entry 2.94 MB (at most 2,955,040); a batch shows 64 columns of a stored copy, and a copy bears 688,127 batches. At target_small_f2, target_small_f2_roomy and target_small_f2_gap: a proof of at most 4,279,884 bytes, a key of at most 1,120,888 and an entry of at most 546,112; 53 columns, 1,235 batches. At target_small_file_f2, whose weight table has another size: at most 6,071,252, 1,904,832 and 1,739,600 bytes; 68 columns, 7,709 batches.

You can make these prints yourself: the call needs no GPU. These prints were not made on the released programs: they were made once, and at target_full_f2, target_small_f2 and target_small_f2_roomy on a second machine too, with the same numbers (reported; compared by program). On the released programs the planning call at target_full_f2 printed the same figures (reported). They are the planner's figures: no size of a proof, a key or an entry on this page was measured on a file ("What has run under it", below, says what has been made).

The second reckoning of a whole parameter file's rounds in this repository, verifier/tests/kit_rounds_second.py, computes single rounds' figures, lists and numbers of positions at target_full_f2 and target_small_f2, about a third of the stored lines; it checks no sum of errors, not the compiled bound and no file's size. For the proximity tests it goes by the formulas of a [technical report](../../docs/glossary.md#technical-report) that is not public and not refereed, with choices and shapes of the programs taken from their headers and marked so; for the committed table's own lines, it takes the formulas of blocks/numbers/blocks.h, so that there it is a second arithmetic, not a second derivation: it would show a slip in the programs' search for the distances or in their numbers, not a wrong formula. Its head says what else it leaves out.

**What the accounting says and does not say.** Each round's error is compared with the target by itself, and the rounds' errors are not added to one another; the planning call prints the stricter readings too, and for every group on what its rounds rest.

At full size:

- each round has at most 2^-128.00 with its work counted, and the least round has 96.23 bits without it;
- 71 rounds of key, entry and proof together hold at the target only because their proof of work is counted, and 68 hold with less than 0.01 bit to spare (25 of the smallness test, 43 with a closeness term);
- added, the rounds' errors give 2^-119.38 with the work and 2^-92.42 with the work counted for nothing;
- against a prover that makes up to 2^L hash calls the compiled bound gives 126.21 bits as one number with the work counted (the printed figure: by the planning call's own line, the largest L such that such a prover, the verifier's calls counted in, succeeds with probability at most its calls over 2^L; with one constant that holds for every parameter file that the programs admit), 96.23 without;
- by a union bound over its columns the smallness test's round has 2^-113.44.

That g bits of work before a verifier's message multiply its round's error by 2^-g is assumed for these programs' hash chains; it is proved for another setting in ePrint 2021/582, Theorem 6 (the planning call says so). So a `YES` leans on that assumption too, in the 71 rounds that hold only by their work.

All these are for one key, one entry and one request's proof: counted jointly for a full batch of N requests, each round of a request's own chains reads log2(N) bits less (6 bits at 64).

Every round that has a closeness term, 255 of a proof, 63 of a key and 23 of an entry, rests on the [theorem on codes](../../docs/glossary.md#theorem-on-codes) (Theorem 4.6 of the full version, ePrint 2025/2055, of the paper by Ben-Sasson, Carmon, Haböck, Kopparty and Saraf published at STOC 2026), and none on the bound within a third of the distance: so does the binding of the weights ("What it rests on", below). At the first three small configurations: 96.23 bits without the work, 38 rounds only by the work; added, 2^-120.75 and 2^-92.73; 126.21 and 96.23; 2^-116.40; 78, 37 and 12 rounds with a closeness term. At target_small_file_f2: 96.04 bits, 38 rounds; 2^-120.57 and 2^-92.62; 126.21 and 96.04; 2^-114.14; 88, 43 and 15.

The rounds' errors and the compiled bound are cited from published work, some of it not peer-reviewed, or argued on paper in the technical report; they are not proved for these programs (the Lean library proves its own verifier's bound, not this one). No figure here is a security level.

**What has run under it, and what has not.** One run at the small size, not on the released programs (reported). At target_small_f2_roomy's file, on synthetic weights, under a seed that cp_commit drew, with the three programs in their default build: a commitment, one request alone, and a service of five batches of 24, which served 120 of 120. Each of the 121 proofs ended a separate whole call of cp_verify with `YES`, status 0.

Of 83 altered files and calls all were rejected: 32 copies of the key, 32 of the [leaf file](../../docs/glossary.md#leaf-file), 16 of a proof, and another reply, another ID and another request; the altered places went by the files' lengths, and none but a file's first byte came near the OOD answers. It tested nothing of hiding.

One run at full size, not on the released programs (reported). At target_full_f2's file, on synthetic weights, under a seed that cp_commit drew, with the same three programs: a commitment, one request alone, and a service of five batches of 24. Each of the 121 proofs ended a separate call of cp_verify with `YES`, status 0.

Of 83 altered files and calls all were rejected: 32 copies of the key, 32 of the leaf file, 16 of a proof, and another reply, another ID and another request. The key, the leaf file and every proof were within the planning call's "at most". It ran once, and it tested nothing of hiding. At the other files, target_full_f2_gap's apart, `cp_verify`'s planning calls have been made (what else ran at target_small_file_f2: "target_small_file_f2: what it is", above; at target_small_f2_gap: its README.md).

Further runs at the small files and at full size are told in [docs/limits.md](../../docs/limits.md#what-has-run). Tests of the three programs with cheating provers and committers are not part of this repository and have not run on the released programs; lying proofs made at full size by test programs which this repository cannot build have been replayed on the released `cp_verify`, which rejected all 57 (docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row LIM-63.1). On other programs the tests ran: three tests with cheating provers at a fixed set without proof of work; two with a cheating committer (ten cases; both tests run by hand: row SO-15.5), at this page's copy with more room; and one edited prover (113 lies), at the default's small form itself. What came of them: docs/limits-all-rows.tsv, row SO-15.5 (reported).

The times, the memory and the sizes that comments in the sources quote are to be taken as seen at the default setting only where a comment says so.

**How the small configurations' lines were chosen.** This repository records no rule by which the first 32 lines of the small files follow from the full size's. What the files themselves show, small beside full size (target_small_f2, target_small_f2_roomy and target_small_f2_gap have one and the same 32 lines; target_small_file_f2 differs from them in the line `table` alone).

Alike: lines 1 to 14 and the line `stages once-ranges`, to the letter; the rate of the first stage's code in every group (1/2 in `link`, 1/4 in `folded` and `once-folded`, 1/16 in the other five); and at both sizes `once-folded`, `once-omega` and `once-own` have the very numbers of `folded`, `omega` and `own`.

Not alike, beside the lengths: above all the numbers of stages. By the lines `stages` the eight groups have, in the files' order, 5, 4, 3, 4, 4, 3, 4 and 3 later stages at full size, and 4, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2 and 3 in the small files. So a run at a small configuration passes through fewer stages than one at full size in seven of the eight groups, and what it shows of the later stages is less. Beyond the first 32 lines, the line `s` is 16 in the small configurations but `target_small_file_f2`, the one that is made for a model's files; it is 15 there and at full size.

A few constants of the full-size parameter file were computed for a public model, Llama 4 Maverick, or settled by work on a fit to it: that `s 15`, the exponent of the scale of the logits, and the value 53,687 of the lines `eps1_lo`, `eps2_lo` and `epsF_lo`. [../../THIRD_PARTY.md](../../THIRD_PARTY.md) ("What depends on Meta's models") lists which of Meta's models these constants and the full-size file of scales, KL.bin, are computed from or may depend on. These constants are fixed public numbers, the same for every provider.

## The examples' requests

run_target_example.sh takes the file own_choices.req of the configuration's folder as its request, if the folder has one. Else it lets ../tools/make_request.py write a window of made-up tokens with r = T - 1 and e = T: no token of the window is covered, and the proof is about the next token only.

For every configuration that is larger than the small examples the script first looks at how much host memory is available, holds it to the full-size figure, which was not measured at the default setting, and starts only where there is enough (so a configuration between the small ones and full size is held to that figure though it may need less); else it ends with one line that says how much it wants and what it found, exit status 2, and creates nothing.

The script opens its logs by name in the work directory: if a process of your own account puts a named pipe at such a name, the script waits there without a message.

Two folders have a file `own_choices.req`. **target_full_f2/own_choices.req**: r = 23, e = 1,024: a made-up prompt of 24 tokens, then 1,000 tokens, each of which is the model's first choice after all the tokens before it. The proof covers each of these 1,000 tokens and y, the token after the window. **target_small_f2_roomy/own_choices.req**: r = 15, e = 64: a prompt of 16 tokens (the first 16 of make_request.py's filling), 48 tokens of the model's own, and y. cp_verify's line `covered:` says so in each call. The other small configurations have no such file: their example's proof is about the last position only. (target_small_f2_gap has two requests of another kind: its README.md.)

Such a request holds the model's own first choices; the integer-model package makes such a reply for any prompt ([../../integrations/vllm/integer_model/README.md](../../integrations/vllm/integer_model/README.md)). From a prompt's and a reply's token ids to a request file (T is 64, 256 or 1,024, by the folder; token 0 fills the window; the proof then covers the reply's tokens and, since e < T, not y; the file holds a person's prompt and reply, so it is made readable for your account only):

```sh
python3 -I -c 'import json, os, struct, sys
one, T = json.load(open(sys.argv[1])), int(sys.argv[3])
p, a = one["prompt_token_ids"], one["reply_token_ids"]
words = p + a + [0] * (T - len(p) - len(a)) + [len(p) - 1, len(p) + len(a) - 1]
new = os.open(sys.argv[2], os.O_WRONLY | os.O_CREAT | os.O_EXCL | os.O_CLOEXEC, 0o600)
with os.fdopen(new, "wb") as f: f.write(struct.pack("<%dI" % (T + 2), *words))' reply.json reply.req 64
```

The two stored files' replies fill the window, and their last word is e = T: so y is covered too. From a file of T tokens as 32-bit little-endian words: `../tools/make_request.py <policy.txt> <request file> <file of tokens> <r> <e>`. Under the exact match a request with one covered token that is not the model's first choice gets no proof: cp_prove writes `<name>.failed`.

**What a request file goes with.** The tokens follow from the weights (`-weights made-up-blocks`: the same in every run), from KL.bin and from the model's lines of policy.txt (from `prof` on). They do not depend on the seed that cp_commit draws, nor on the lines of policy.txt before `prof`.

**How the two files were made.** The small file: by the integer-model package, from the prompt, on the made-up weights, under the folder's policy.txt and KL.bin. As a check the prover's own forward pass was run without a proof on the same weights: at every covered position it gave the same token as its first choice after the tokens before it (on a tie it takes the lowest token id). The file was served and accepted by the default build, through run_target_example.sh, once.

The full-size file: in the same way and with the same check. It is, byte for byte, the request of the stored example `examples/full-size`, which cp_prove served once† (reported) and cp_verify accepts.

## The three files

policy.txt: the parameter file. At full size policy.txt's first 32 lines are blocks/frame/examples/record.f2.txt, byte for byte; the library's other parameter files (blocks/numbers/settings) are files of their own and do not follow these configurations.

KL.bin: the scales. THIRD_PARTY.md lists on which of Meta's models the full-size configuration's scales may depend.

layout.txt: which array lies in which rows of which link's message; it follows from the other two files, and is made by `cp_verify -plan arrays` and `prover/tools/pack_layout.py --arrays` with the number 17 at the small configurations and 22 at full size (log2 of the link group's message length): [../docs/layout.md](../docs/layout.md), "How to generate a layout file", has the commands, which replace no file. Made by these two commands at target_full_f2, target_small_f2, target_small_f2_roomy and target_small_f2_gap, it came out as each folder has it (once, by program)†.

**The table's OOD answers** (in docs/format/: "The table's record"). Every stored table has an out-of-domain sample of its own (the line `record 1`), whose answers, one for each row, are in the [key file](../../docs/glossary.md#key-file) (key.bin) and in each leaf file, after the root. The four lines `group once-..` put the committer's groups under rule 1 (up to the Johnson radius), three of them with the stage lines of a batch's groups. The fifth number of `table`, the radius of the one-time test, is 0: the table's distances are derived from the target.

- What it rests on. The truth of an accepted request and the binding of the weights both rest on the theorem on codes, and at the default setting both lean on counted proof of work in the rounds that draw positions.
  - *The theorem and the further steps.* (That counting is itself assumed for the hash chains: "What the accounting says and does not say", above.) The closeness rounds' bound rests on a theorem from the full version (ePrint 2025/2055, Theorem 4.6: not in the proceedings; that version gives an outline of its proof) of a paper published at STOC 2026, which the programs' accounting takes as given. A statement on codes held to follow from that theorem, in its case of two words, is proved in this repository's Lean library, by way of another formalization's theorem and a comparison of bounds; the paper's own form of the bound is not proved there, and that the library's statement follows from the paper's, and that these programs' parameters meet the theorem's conditions, rest on reading, not on a proof (lean/LIMITS.md, L15). We have checked no proof of the paper's own form of the bound ([what has been checked of it, and what has not](../../docs/limits.md#what-the-theorem-assumes)). The bound also rests on further steps argued in our technical report alone. That our parameters meet the theorem's conditions is our own reckoning, with no bits to spare.

    The further steps are three: no factor for the interleaved rows; a fold of 2^v rows counts v closeness terms; one list size more for the repeated mask code beyond a third of the distance. [docs/limits.md](../../docs/limits.md#what-the-theorem-assumes) tells all this at length.

    Binding is meant at the default setting in a modified sense, in which the hash itself supplies the OOD seeds. That sense does not imply the binding definition that the report cites, and the report's written argument for that sense covers a commitment ID whose hashed string does not take the OOD seeds: the programs' string takes them, and that argument does not cover that string, so of the programs' own commitment no binding theorem is proved ([binding](../../docs/glossary.md#binding)).
  - *The technical report.* The technical report's general theorems cover the proximity tests' use of out-of-domain samples. Two test inputs under `prover/tests/configurations/` count no proof of work. For that case a corollary like the one stated below for the default setting, under the theorem on codes, with a proposition for what is left without that theorem, has not been checked independently, and it has not been checked that either test input meets its conditions. Readings have found faults in both statements: sentences false as worded, and one printed upper limit too low. That the report's text as it stands is free of them rests on the report's own account; part of one of its proofs has been read by nobody but its author (rows SO-2.2, LIM-14.3).

    The technical report states, for the default setting with the differences that its appendix names, a corollary with counted proof of work and a proposition on what would remain without the theorem on codes. If that theorem failed, the programs' accounting would rest on one other cited bound, itself unverified and not wholly independent of it; what would remain is, by the report's calculation on paper, fewer bits than the target (the size: row LIM-17.2), for the truth of an accepted request and for binding alike, and the report does not call it firm; the Lean verifier's own bound does not take the theorem on codes as given.
  - *What was compared with the programs.* Of the shipped parameter files, only the full-size default was compared with the report's setting: once, against a planning call†, which checks no proof.

    The file states the setting with differences at a few places, which the report names (one mask code half as long in one offline group, in which one stage opens 25 positions against the report's 28; lists smaller by 2 to 5 at four further stages; a few more rounds). The report draws no level from these: its figures are for its own values. Beyond that comparison it has not been checked that a file which the verifier accepts meets the corollary's conditions.
